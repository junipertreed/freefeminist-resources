In the past few years, liberal feminism has often been spoken of derisively, as a shallow form of feminism. Liberal feminism is part of the liberal political philosophy, which was developed in the 17th century and generally focused on the human right to liberty, or freedom. The Glorious Revolution of England, the American Revolution, and the French Revolution were all based in liberal political philosophy. The Haitian Revolution was also inspiried by belief in the human right to individual liberty and freedom, but liberal thinkers of past and present do not always consider it as a liberal revolution.  

This is an example of a main critque of liberal feminism: lack of inclusion. The same values of freedom were not extended to all. Liberal feminism focuses on reform and granting political rights to achieve social and economic equality, but for women in groups where the men do not have access to these rights, it can be unhelpful. Liberal feminism has been critqued for excluding women of color and queer women in particular. Both of those groups have developed robust feminist movements and theories that take their own experiences, either as a group or as individual people, into account.
